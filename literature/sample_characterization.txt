Sample Characterization for Nanoindentation Studies
===================================================

SURFACE PREPARATION
-------------------
Quality surface preparation is critical for accurate nanoindentation:

Metallographic preparation:
1. Mounting: Cold-mount if heat-sensitive; hot-mount for routine work
2. Grinding: Sequential grits (240 → 400 → 600 → 800 → 1200)
3. Polishing: Diamond suspension (6 μm → 3 μm → 1 μm)
4. Final polish: Colloidal silica (0.05 μm) or alumina
5. Cleaning: Ultrasonic in ethanol, dry with compressed air

Surface roughness requirements:
- Ra < 5 nm: ideal for shallow indents (<200 nm)
- Ra < 20 nm: acceptable for standard indents (200-1000 nm)
- Ra < 100 nm: acceptable for deep indents (>1 μm)

SAMPLE MOUNTING
--------------
For accurate indentation:
- Rigid mounting required to prevent sample motion
- Parallel surfaces essential for proper contact mechanics
- Typical mounting options:
  - Crystal bond wax: temperature sensitive, easy removal
  - Cyanoacrylate (super glue): rigid, harder removal
  - Double-sided tape: quick, but may introduce compliance

Sample thickness considerations:
- Minimum 10x maximum indent depth to avoid substrate effects
- For 500 nm indents: sample or coating >5 μm thick
- Thin films: use substrate correction methods

MICROSTRUCTURE CHARACTERIZATION
------------------------------
Correlating indentation with microstructure requires:

Optical microscopy:
- Identify phase distributions, grain sizes
- Mark reference points for indent location
- Typical magnification: 50-500x for overview

Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM):
- High resolution phase identification
- EBSD for grain orientation mapping
- EDS for compositional analysis
- Correlate indent locations with microstructure

Pre-test imaging strategy:
1. Low magnification overview (100-200x)
2. Identify regions of interest (phases, boundaries)
3. Mark coordinates relative to fiducial markers
4. Plan indent array positions

THERMAL EFFECTS
---------------
Temperature influences measurements:
- Thermal drift during measurement causes errors
- Allow samples to equilibrate (30+ minutes)
- Monitor ambient temperature stability (±0.5°C)
- Use drift correction algorithms

Elevated temperature testing:
- Requires specialized heated stages
- Account for tip thermal expansion
- Material properties change with temperature

ENVIRONMENTAL CONSIDERATIONS
----------------------------
Humidity effects:
- High humidity can cause water meniscus at contact
- Oxidation of fresh surfaces
- Recommendation: RH 30-50% for most materials

Vibration isolation:
- Acoustic and mechanical vibrations cause noise
- Use isolation tables for sub-100 nm indents
- Night operation may have lower vibration

MULTI-PHASE MATERIALS
--------------------
Challenges with heterogeneous samples:
- Phase size vs indent size determines averaging
- If phase size < 3x indent diameter: composite response
- Target: phase size > 5x indent size for phase-specific H

Identification strategies:
- Pre-test SEM/optical imaging of phases
- Etching to reveal microstructure
- In-situ optical microscopy during indentation
- Post-test SEM of indent locations

Example phase size requirements:
- For 1 μm deep indents with ~7 μm contact diameter:
  - Phase size >35 μm for clean measurement
  - Phase size <20 μm gives mixed response

THIN FILM AND COATING ANALYSIS
------------------------------
Substrate influence rules:
- Film properties dominate when h < 0.1t (h=depth, t=thickness)
- Composite response when 0.1t < h < t
- Substrate dominated when h > t

Recommended practice:
- Maximum indent depth: 10% of film thickness
- For 1 μm film: maximum 100 nm indent depth
- Use continuous stiffness measurement (CSM) for depth profiling

Film-substrate systems to consider:
- Soft film on hard substrate: pile-up likely
- Hard film on soft substrate: sink-in likely, possible cracking
- Similar modulus: smoother transition

STATISTICAL SAMPLING
-------------------
Minimum indent requirements:
- Homogeneous material: 5-10 indents
- Phase-specific measurement: 10-15 per phase
- Mapping heterogeneity: 25-100+ indents

Sampling strategies:
- Random sampling: unbiased property estimate
- Grid sampling: spatial distribution mapping
- Targeted sampling: specific features (boundaries, phases)

Reporting guidelines:
- State sample preparation method
- Report number of indents and locations
- Include uncertainty estimates
- Note any anomalous indents excluded and reason
