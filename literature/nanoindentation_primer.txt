Nanoindentation: Fundamentals and Best Practices
================================================

MECHANICAL PROPERTY MEASUREMENT
-------------------------------
Nanoindentation is a technique for measuring local mechanical properties including:
- Hardness (H): Resistance to plastic deformation, typically in GPa
- Reduced modulus (Er) and Young's modulus (E): Elastic stiffness
- Strain rate sensitivity and time-dependent properties

For metals and alloys:
- Hardness typically ranges 1-15 GPa depending on composition
- Steel: H ~ 2-8 GPa, E ~ 200 GPa
- Aluminum alloys: H ~ 0.5-2 GPa, E ~ 70 GPa
- Nickel-based superalloys: H ~ 3-7 GPa, E ~ 180-220 GPa
- Titanium alloys: H ~ 3-5 GPa, E ~ 100-120 GPa

For ceramics and hard coatings:
- Hardness typically 10-40 GPa
- Diamond-like carbon: H ~ 20-80 GPa
- TiN coatings: H ~ 20-25 GPa
- Al2O3: H ~ 20-30 GPa

INDENTATION SIZE EFFECT (ISE)
-----------------------------
At shallow indentation depths (<500 nm), measured hardness often increases with decreasing depth due to:
- Strain gradient plasticity
- Surface oxide layers
- Polishing damage
- Pile-up or sink-in effects

Recommendation: Use depths >100 nm to minimize ISE artifacts; depths of 200-500 nm are typical for reliable bulk property measurement.

SPACING REQUIREMENTS
-------------------
To avoid interaction between adjacent indentations:
- Minimum spacing: 3-5x maximum indentation depth
- For 500 nm deep indents: minimum 2.5-5 μm spacing
- For 1 μm deep indents: minimum 5-10 μm spacing
- Near grain boundaries: additional spacing may be needed

Grid pattern considerations:
- 5x5 grids with 5 μm spacing cover 20x20 μm areas
- For phase mapping, align grids with microstructural features
- Rotate grids to sample multiple grain orientations

PHASE AND MICROSTRUCTURE EFFECTS
--------------------------------
Different phases exhibit distinct mechanical signatures:
- Ferrite (α-Fe): H ~ 2-3 GPa, softer phase in steels
- Martensite: H ~ 6-8 GPa, hard phase from rapid cooling
- Pearlite: H ~ 3-4 GPa, lamellar ferrite + cementite
- Austenite (γ): H ~ 2-4 GPa depending on composition

Grain boundary effects:
- Hardness often increases near grain boundaries (Hall-Petch)
- Grain interiors may be 10-30% softer than boundaries
- Triple junctions can show anomalous properties

Precipitate effects:
- Hard precipitates increase local hardness
- Precipitate-free zones (PFZ) near boundaries can be softer

EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN RECOMMENDATIONS
-----------------------------------
For hypothesis testing about spatial property variations:
1. Conduct initial survey with coarse grid (10+ μm spacing)
2. Identify regions of interest from optical/SEM images
3. Fine grids in identified regions (3-5 μm spacing)
4. Statistical analysis requires minimum 9-25 indents per region

Expected property differences for confirmation:
- Phase discrimination: >15-20% hardness difference
- Grain boundary effects: >10% hardness variation
- Composition gradients: continuous trends across positions

Load selection guidelines:
- Lower loads (1-5 mN): surface sensitivity, coating analysis
- Medium loads (5-20 mN): bulk property, standard testing  
- Higher loads (20-100 mN): averaging over microstructure

COMMON EXPERIMENTAL HYPOTHESES
------------------------------
1. "Hardness varies spatially due to phase distribution"
   - Test: Compare hardness in phase A vs phase B regions
   - Success: Statistically significant H difference (t-test p<0.05)
   - Failure: Uniform H regardless of location

2. "Grain boundaries are mechanically distinct from grain interiors"
   - Test: Indents at boundaries vs centers of grains
   - Success: >10% H difference with consistent trend
   - Failure: Random variation without boundary correlation

3. "Compositional gradient causes property gradient"
   - Test: Linear transect of indents across gradient
   - Success: Monotonic H trend correlating with composition
   - Failure: No correlation between position and properties

4. "Heat treatment changed local mechanical response"
   - Test: Compare same locations before/after treatment
   - Success: Systematic H change in expected direction
   - Failure: Changes within measurement uncertainty

DATA INTERPRETATION
------------------
Sources of measurement uncertainty:
- Instrument noise: typically ±2-5% for well-calibrated systems
- Surface roughness: can cause 5-10% scatter
- Tip wear: calibrate regularly with fused silica reference
- Thermal drift: perform drift corrections, use temperature-stable environment

Statistical recommendations:
- Report mean ± standard deviation for each region
- Compare regions using appropriate statistical tests
- Consider coefficient of variation (CV) for relative comparison
- CV < 5%: highly uniform material
- CV 5-10%: typical polycrystalline variation
- CV > 15%: significant microstructural heterogeneity
